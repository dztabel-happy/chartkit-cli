# 26 Composite Solar Curtailment Small Multiples

## Visual Strategy

A uniform 3×4 grid of the same day-profile chart, one cell per month, reading left-to-right in calendar order. Three lines per cell carry the whole story: the green available envelope (what the plant could deliver), the dashed blue forecast (what was scheduled), and the red actual output (what the grid accepted) — with the curtailment window shaded so the gap has a cause attached. The y-scale is shared, so summer and winter cells are directly comparable; the x-axis appears once per column and the legend once for the figure. Each panel title carries the month plus its one headline number (curtailment rate), which turns the grid into a scannable table of results without adding any chrome.

## Use When

The same chart must be compared across an ordered set of facets: months, cohorts, sites, regimes. The composite earns its place when the *pattern within each facet* (the daily shape) and the *drift across facets* (seasonal deepening) are both part of the claim. Per-facet interval shading fits recurring windows: curtailment periods, incident windows, promotion campaigns.

## Avoid

Avoid composites whose cells differ in chart type or density — that is a report-layout job for separate figures. Avoid more than a few series per cell; at cell size, extra lines become noise. Avoid per-cell legends, per-cell y labels, or unshared scales by default. Avoid cramming per-cell annotations; one number in the title is the budget.
